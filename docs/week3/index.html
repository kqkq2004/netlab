What I learned

Describe your design: why these routers, LANs and links, and how you chose the addresses.

Four routers in a ring (r1-r2-r3-r4-r1), so every pair of routers has two paths. r3 is a site with two LANs, h3 on 10.66.32.0/24 and h4 on 10.66.33.0/24, placed inside one /22 so the other routers need just one route for both. The other LANs are numbered after their router (10.66.1, .2, .4) and links after the routers they join; the r3-r4 link is 43, not 34, because 10.66.34.0 is inside the /22.

Which route summarises several LANs? Which networks does it cover, why is it correct, and what else would it attract if you added a network inside its range?

10.66.32.0/22 on r1, r2 and r4 covers 10.66.32.0 to 10.66.35.255, which holds h3's and h4's LANs. It's correct because both are behind r3 and each route's next hop leads towards r3; summary.txt shows r1 sending 10.66.32.10 and 10.66.33.10 to the same next hop, 10.66.12.2.

If I added 10.66.34.0/24 on another router, the /22 would still pull its traffic to r3, which has no route for it, unless a more specific /24 was added, since longest-prefix match prefers it.

When you took a link down, what happened, and why couldn't your routes recover even though another path existed? What would have recovered?

I took r1-r2 down. h1's trace to h3 stopped at r1, and r1 reported the network as unreachable: Linux deleted the static routes through that link, and nothing replaced them. The path r1-r4-r3 still worked, but static routes only know what I typed; nothing detects a failure and recomputes. A routing protocol such as OSPF would have recovered, or a backup static route via r4 with a higher metric.

In your network, what was the control plane? Who worked out the routes, and what does that mean for a network of 40 routers?

I was the control plane: I worked out every route by hand and wrote them in network.yaml. build_routed.py only applied them, after check_paths() checked every host pair for loops and missing routes. With 40 routers that means hundreds of routes to keep correct after every change or failure, so a real network needs a routing protocol or a controller as its control plane.
